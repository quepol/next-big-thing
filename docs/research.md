# Research, the venue page and Backstage

How the setlist.fm research works and how the two pages built on it behave. Summary in `CLAUDE.md`; history in closed #4; backlog in #11 (weekly refresh, side projects, momentum), #5 (watchlist) and #7 (small rooms Ticketmaster misses).

## Data sources

### Ticketmaster (the only event feed)

Bandsintown's city feed is **permanently blocked by Cloudflare** from any server or CI IP. All request approaches (plain curl, mobile UA, JSON accept header) return 403. The Bandsintown REST API (`rest.bandsintown.com`) requires a `BANDSINTOWN_APP_ID` that is no longer freely issued. A 2026-10-18 probe (`research/bandsintown_probe.py`, results in `research/bandsintown_probe.json`) confirmed the stored `BANDSINTOWN_APP_ID` gets HTTP 401 on artist past-events too, so Bandsintown is out for history and billing. Billing going forward comes from Ticketmaster: daily event snapshots keep lineup order, headliner first (154 of 526 events on 2026-10-18 list more than one act).

**Ticketmaster Discovery API** (`app.ticketmaster.com/discovery/v2/events`) is the city feed. It has a free key (`TICKETMASTER_API_KEY` secret), supports lat/long radius search with `classificationName=music`, and includes TicketWeb (used by most small Toronto clubs). Coverage check result: 557 events in the Toronto region for the next 90 days, ~28% with opener data in `_embedded.attractions`, and all four small-venue spot-checks (Lee's Palace, Horseshoe, Baby G, Monarch Tavern) were present with correct lineups.

Gaps: DICE, Eventbrite, and venues' own box offices are not covered. The Baby G had only 2 events (likely sells through its own site). These are acceptable for now.

The `nbt/sources/ticketmaster.py` module includes a 50-venue capacity lookup table (keyed by TM venue ID) used to compute `venue_bonus`.

### setlist.fm

`nbt/sources/setlistfm.py` fetches all attended setlists (111 total, 6 pages) for user `quepol`. 7-day cache; 1s between pages; 3× retry on 429s.

Three uses:
1. **Attended artists as seeds** — 11 artists seen live but with <3 scrobble-days (Billy Corgan, Iron Maiden, PJ Harvey, John Cameron Mitchell, etc.) are injected into the taste graph at seed weight 0.4. Uncontaminated by kids' listening.
2. **Attended artists as loved** — merged into the `loved` set, so they trigger out-of-town alerts (Hamilton, Buffalo, Orillia, Kitchener, Guelph, London) and bill_bonus. Since 2026-10-26 `loved` blends recency: the top 200 by days played if still played since 2023, plus 8+ days since 2023, plus loves and every attended artist (attended artists need no play days). See CLAUDE.md, Product.
3. **Venue novelty** — picks at venues the user has never attended get a "you've never been to X" reason. Matching is fuzzy: TM adds "The " prefixes and city suffixes ("The Danforth Music Hall", "History Toronto"); setlist.fm uses shorter names ("Danforth Music Hall", "History"). The `_venue_attended()` helper in `scoring.py` handles this with substring containment on the core name.

The **attended ledger** is included in `digest.json` and rendered on Backstage's Attended tab: every logged show (back to 1991) with current listener counts.

## Research jobs (`research/`)

Each job has its own workflow that runs on pushes to the dev branch, triggered only by a change to its own script, because every run spends the shared setlist.fm budget. Each one writes JSON into `site/data/` and commits it back to the branch.

- `venue_overlap.py` (`venue-research.yml`) writes `venue_research.json`. It pulls setlist.fm history since Oct 2023 for the user's SF rooms and for all of Toronto. From that it works out the Toronto rooms ranked against the city average, each SF room's closest Toronto rooms, the shared artists, the bands that came back to Toronto with co-bill billing, and taste tiers for every shared artist (`add_tiers`, using the pipeline's `Taste` and cutoffs). It restores (never saves) the daily API cache. The Last.fm cache has its own step.
- `ladder_study.py` (`ladder-study.yml`) writes `ladder_study.json`: Bay Area and Toronto history for The National, Arcade Fire, The Decemberists, Bon Iver and Guster. It feeds Backstage's "for pace" box.
- `bandsintown_probe.py` was a one-off check. Its output in `bandsintown_probe.json` confirmed Bandsintown is unusable.

## Venue page ("SF overlap")

Titled "SF overlap" (the file is still `venues.html`). It opens with a tongue-in-cheek disclaimer that this is the raw research behind Backstage, then sticky section links (Findings, SF rooms, Twins, Toronto rooms, Larger rooms, Coming back, Played both).

Research page: `site/venues.html` + `venues.js` (closed issue #4) render `data/venue_research.json`, written by `research/venue_overlap.py` via `.github/workflows/venue-research.yml` (runs on a push that changes `research/venue_overlap.py` on the dev branch; resumable through an API cache). Toronto capacities for that page are in `data/toronto_capacity.json`, keyed by lowercased setlist.fm venue name. Not linked from the main page. It also has a "Coming back to Toronto" list: shared artists with 2+ Toronto visits (30+ days apart). Last.fm listeners are looked up with typographic quotes normalised, because "Arm’s Length" otherwise matches a near-empty page. Counts under 3,000 are flagged as a possible wrong match. Headline vs support: setlist.fm records no billing. Where other acts were logged the same night, the research job takes the act with the most Last.fm listeners as the headliner (about 80% of comeback shows; half turned out to be support slots). Otherwise the page falls back to the user's room-size rule: Massey Hall size (2,765) or smaller counts as headlining; bigger rooms and labelled spaces (Arena, Festival, Outdoor) count as likely support. Support shows are greyed with a tag and left off the ladder, and "moving up" uses headline shows only. The five-band aughts ladder study (`research/ladder_study.py`, `.github/workflows/ladder-study.yml`, output `site/data/ladder_study.json`) feeds Backstage's "for pace" box; its first run hit setlist.fm's daily limit on 10-18, and it completed on 10-19. The user frequented these SF rooms; they weren't chosen as a taste test, so no control-room comparison is planned.

## Backstage (was Explore)

Backstage: `site/explore.html` + `explore.js` (the file keeps its old name so links still work), linked both ways with the venue page. The site splits into recommendations (the picks page) and the data behind them (Backstage). Four tabs: **The Climb** (the default), **Rivers**, **Ledger** and **Attended**. The URL hash (`#climb`, `#rivers`, `#ledger`, `#attended`) picks the tab; the old `#rooms` opens Rivers. Band lookup is a search box in the header, and every band card opens as a sheet over the page (a bottom sheet on phones), whether from the search, the climb list or the rivers. The user asked for an exploratory view: phone first, desktop can be better, and a guess-the-band game is deliberately left out. Sections:
    - The Climb (the main feature) is a ranked list, one row per band. The user found the original all-bands chart unreadable on a phone because no line was labelled. Each row shows the band's headline steps ("Monarch Tavern 250 → Great Hall 600"), how much the room size grew and over what time, the next Toronto date from the calendar, and a tiny ladder graphic. Tapping a row opens its shows with billing. Rows are sorted by growth in room size. A "for pace" box above the list shows the aughts bands' time from a small room to Warfield size, from `data/ladder_study.json`. The old staircase chart is still available in a collapsed section ("Chart of every climb") and only renders when opened.
    - Band lookup, which also uses the attended ledger. The band card follows the picks page's card style without the date tile: the strength badge (pips, label, raw score), SF rooms, chips (tier, listeners, days played, moving up / came back, seen, next date) and a "why" line with similarity weights. The score is the live pick when the band is in today's digest; otherwise it's rebuilt from the research tier with the pipeline's formula, minus the venue, bill and growth bonuses (the tooltip says which). Every band's Toronto shows are listed with dates; the comeback list adds billing. The card ends with the main page's listen links (SoundCloud search via the music note, and YouTube) and, when the band has an upcoming Toronto show on the calendar, a Tickets link (the date and room are in the chips). The old "Show on The Climb" button was removed when the chart was folded away.
    - Room rivers: SF → Toronto streams; widths are raw counts. Tap a room or a stream to open a band panel of small cards (listeners, tier, next date, moving up / came back / seen). The filters are All, the three main-page tiers, Playing soon, Moving up, Came back and You've seen. Tiers come from the research job (`add_tiers` in `research/venue_overlap.py`), which scores the shared bands with the pipeline's Taste model and cutoffs; the tier filters only appear once that data exists.
    - Ledger and Attended: plain tables from the digest, moved here from the picks page. Dates are short ("Oct 19" this year, "Jul 2019" before) and listener counts compact, so the tables fit a phone. Attended names the city only outside Toronto.
    - Your rooms (now on the picks page): upcoming shows at followed rooms, ranked. The score is the best act's taste fit plus fixed bonuses (`WEIGHTS` in `rooms.js`, shared with the picks page's Your rooms tab): you know them, you've heard them, you've seen them, moving up, came back, played your SF rooms. Followed rooms are saved in `localStorage` as `nbt.rooms`. Defaults are the user's picks: Phoenix, Mod Club, Opera House, Horseshoe, Concert Hall, Adelaide Hall, Drake Underground. Followed rooms filter and sort; they never add to a show's score. The data comes from the digest's new `calendar` field (every upcoming show, each act with affinity, days played and listeners), made by `nbt.scoring.calendar()`, which drops nothing at the tier cutoffs. Until a digest has it, the page rebuilds what it can from the picks.
      On the picks page, Your rooms is the last tab, with the tier cards and the sort menu. "Followed rooms" is the default; the dropdown starts with "All rooms" and then every other room. A card's badge is the show score on the shared 1–10 scale (`SCALES.rooms` in `ladder.js`: 0.15 is 1.0, 0.30 is 5.0 (Good), 0.50 is 8.0 (Strong), 0.90 is 10). Below 1.0 there's no label or pips, just the number, because this tab lists every show, quiet nights included. On Oct 22, 10 of 104 followed-room shows were Strong and about 70 had no label. Strong can come from bonuses alone (TALK: taste fit 0.05, but known, keeps coming back and played an SF room). A show scores on the best act anywhere on the bill. When that's an opener, its name is bold in the "with …" line (`.where .lead`), so the card shows who to arrive early for. The tier pill names the tier when the show is also a pick. The pink edge is only for a Strong *pick*, never a Strong room show. The why lines show similarity weights and each bonus with its weight. The old room header's research (closest SF rooms, climbers a room launched) was dropped in the move.
    D3 7.9.0 is self-hosted in `site/vendor/` (ISC licence alongside). Billing and ladder rules shared by the pages live in `site/ladder.js`.
